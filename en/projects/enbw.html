Quarterly planning and onsite weeks

EnBW Trading IT

Bringing strategic goals, team roadmaps and dependencies together – so teams and leadership can plan their initiatives jointly and make timely decisions.

EnBW Trading IT · Enterprise agile coaching and temporary release train engineering · Cross-team planning and collaboration

When many teams deliver, a shared direction is needed.

Different initiatives were running in parallel in Trading IT. Teams worked on their own tasks and roadmaps, but were often dependent on each other. For cross-team coordination, it was not enough for each team to plan well on its own. Strategic priorities, team roadmaps, dependencies and open decisions needed to be considered together.

The challenge was therefore not just to create a plan. What was needed was a way of working in which teams, responsible people and management could come together regularly, align their plans and incorporate new insights as work progressed.

My task: turning an idea into an established planning routine.

I worked in the EnBW Trading IT setting over several phases and in different roles – initially in cross-organisational agile development, and temporarily also as a Release Train Engineer. A substantial part of my responsibility was to help shape and further develop the cross-team quarterly planning process and embed it in everyday work.

This involved more than facilitating individual meetings: I designed and prepared formats, brought teams and responsible people together for planning, supported coordination and worked to ensure the agreed processes and feedback loops were actually used. The operational RTE role helped sustain the jointly developed way of working beyond its introduction.

From individual roadmaps to a shared planning rhythm.

It mattered to me that planning should not remain a one-off major event, but become a recurring exchange between strategic direction and teams' actual work.

  1. 01

    Making priorities and dependencies visible

    Strategic goals, upcoming initiatives and team roadmaps were brought together. Dependencies and risks were to become visible early, so those involved would not encounter conflicting plans only during implementation.

  2. 02

    Planning together – deliberately also on site

    Quarterly planning and cross-team onsite weeks gave coordination a regular framework. With prepared agendas, clear questions and suitable exchange formats, teams and leadership could clarify priorities, discuss interfaces and align the next steps together.

  3. 03

    Feeding back decisions and experience

    Status dashboards, dependency overviews and regular feedback to management complemented planning. New insights, bottlenecks and open decisions could thus become visible and inform further coordination.

A planning process that lives on in everyday work.

What mattered was the connection between levels: from strategic priority to concrete coordination between teams – and back to the people responsible for making decisions.

What emerged from this.

The most important progress was that a cross-team planning approach became a regularly used way of working. Those involved had shared opportunities and foundations for aligning priorities, roadmaps and dependencies.

Established quarterly planning

Quarterly planning and onsite weeks were established and used as repeatable, cross-team formats – not just described as a concept.

Greater transparency for shared decisions

Team roadmaps, status dashboards and dependencies were connected. Feedback from the collaboration confirms improved planning quality and earlier visibility of risks and coordination needs.

Better coordination between IT teams, business departments and management

The shared planning and feedback formats created a more reliable basis for coordination between IT teams, business departments and management. Participants described the collaboration as more structured and practical.

About the collaboration

What I take from this experience.

A good strategy only becomes effective when people can translate it into shared decisions and concrete work. This does not necessarily require more processes. It requires clear goals, suitable coordination and a willingness to learn from everyday work.

This experience shapes my work at mehr.wert today: we connect strategic direction with viable ways of working and support their introduction so teams and leadership can actually use them.

Similar challenges in your IT organisation?

When many teams work on shared initiatives, it is worth looking at how goals, planning and delivery currently come together. We would be happy to discuss what could help in your situation.
