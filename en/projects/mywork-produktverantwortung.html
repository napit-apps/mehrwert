OWN PRODUCT · MARCEL RUSS

myWork – Mobile time tracking

An iOS app for tracking working hours and projects – which I developed and took responsibility for myself, from the first idea over many years.

iOS software product · Concept, development, operation and ongoing improvement

A personal need became a software product.

The idea for myWork arose from a desire to make tracking working hours and projects easier. What began as a personal task developed into a mobile application during my studies, which I published and subsequently continued to improve.

What mattered was not only that the app fulfilled its tasks. It needed to remain easy to use in everyday life, accommodate new requirements and be maintained reliably across several generations of technology. myWork thus gave me early practical experience of long-term product responsibility.

With myWork, my responsibility does not end at release.

I conceived, designed and developed myWork myself. Architecture, technical implementation and publication are part of this, as are maintenance, quality assurance, support and decisions about new features or necessary adaptations.

Unlike a time-limited project, I continue to live with the consequences of earlier decisions myself. Something implemented quickly today may be expensive to maintain tomorrow. Something technically elegant must also prove itself in users' daily lives. For me, these two perspectives are inseparable.

Developing a product means bringing many perspectives together.

Over the years, more than just the app has changed. Platform technologies, expectations of usability and requirements for technical maintenance also continue to evolve.

  1. 01

    Designing easy-to-use features

    The starting point is the user's task: recording time and projects without unnecessary detours. This creates requirements for information structure, usability and how features work together – not just technical implementation.

  2. 02

    Developing architecture and technologies

    myWork has been maintained across several generations of iOS technology. This included development with Objective-C and Swift and work with Apple technologies for local and cloud-related data storage. Every enhancement must fit the existing product and its data.

  3. 03

    Considering operation, quality and feedback

    Publication starts the next part of the work: understanding problems, assessing feedback, providing support and planning improvements. Experiences from actual use thus feed back into product and technology decisions.

Use becomes experience. Experience becomes further development.

A software product is not a one-off deliverable. It evolves through interaction with its users and the available technical possibilities.

What has emerged from developing my own product.

An independently developed iOS product

An idea became a published app for mobile time and project tracking, for which I was responsible for both the concept and technical implementation.

Long-term technical responsibility

The application has been developed and supported over years. Technical decisions therefore needed to be viable not only for the next release, but also for maintenance and future changes.

Direct contact with the user perspective

Operation and support reveal where software works in everyday life and where it raises questions. This feedback feeds into ongoing product work.

Why this experience shapes my consulting work today.

Taking responsibility for your own software over many years changes how you view technical decisions. Architecture and maintainability, usability and operating costs, new features and existing dependencies cannot be assessed in isolation.

I bring this product perspective to mehr.wert's work. In IT strategy and modernisation, I ask not only what is technically possible, but also which solution can be operated, changed and economically sustained over the long term.

Would you like to keep your software fit for the future?

Whether you are developing an existing application further or need to make fundamental technology decisions: considering the entire lifecycle helps you choose an appropriate path.
