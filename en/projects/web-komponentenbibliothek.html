PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE · MARCEL RUSS

Shared UI components for web applications

HUGO BOSS

When several teams develop web applications, shared technical foundations help align design and further development.

HUGO BOSS · Web development · Angular · Technical leadership

Multiple applications need more than individual good solutions.

Web applications evolve over many years. When several teams work on them, different interfaces, individual components and technical decisions can emerge, each plausible in its own context. Across teams, this makes it harder to design and develop similar functions consistently.

Work at HUGO BOSS focused on a shared foundation for reusable UI components. The question was not just which components made technical sense. It was equally important to understand how different teams could make sense of a shared solution and incorporate it into their work.

Technical leadership also means helping shape shared foundations.

During my employment at HUGO BOSS, I supported web and mobile development in a technical leadership role. In the work on an Angular component library, I brought this perspective to discussions about shared technical foundations.

The aim was to consider requirements from different applications and teams together and support the goal of a reusable solution. The development work took place within the technical organisation at the time, rather than as a separate client engagement for mehr.wert.

A shared approach emerges between architecture and collaboration.

Reusable components are helpful only when their technical design matches the teams' actual needs. Three perspectives were essential.

  1. 01

    Identify shared requirements

    Similar interfaces and functions offer opportunities for shared UI components. Differences between applications must remain visible so that reuse does not create unnecessary constraints.

  2. 02

    Think through a technical foundation together

    Work on an Angular component library brought together technical and design questions. I contributed to aligning the shared vision and the perspective across teams.

  3. 03

    Treat collaboration as part of the technical solution

    A shared library affects several development teams: decisions on changes, maintenance and usage must be understandable. Coordination is therefore part of architecture work, not merely an organisational addition.

One shared component. Multiple application contexts.

The principle behind a component library is simple: recurring interface functions are not designed independently in every application.

What this experience shows.

At HUGO BOSS, work was carried out on a shared Angular component library. I participated in technical coordination and alignment across teams, bringing the perspective of leading web and mobile teams.

This experience shows how closely technical standardisation and collaboration are linked. Whether a shared library delivers lasting value depends not only on the quality of its components, but also on how well it fits the requirements and working practices of the teams involved.

What makes this experience relevant to today's IT decisions.

Reuse and standardisation are not ends in themselves. They can support long-term maintainability and simplify technical decisions. But they can also create new dependencies within an organisation if shared solutions fail to meet actual needs.

At mehr.wert, we therefore consider technical foundations alongside product goals and organisation. A suitable architecture must not only be developed; the teams involved must also be able to take responsibility for it and maintain it.

Do your teams need shared technical foundations?

When multiple applications and teams solve similar problems, it is worth looking at reuse, architecture and the collaboration required.
