PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE · MARCEL RUSS

Mobile trade-fair information using iBeacon technology

Bosch Power Tools

Provide information on a smartphone that matches the visitor's location within an exhibition stand, helping visitors discover relevant content where they need it.

Mobile application · iBeacon · Technical design and project coordination

Information should appear where it is relevant.

An exhibition stand presents many products and topics in a small space. Visitors move between areas and want information about what currently interests them. A conventional overview can help, but does not automatically relate information to their location.

This led to the idea of connecting a mobile exhibition experience more closely with its surroundings: relevant information would become available on visitors' smartphones when they approached the corresponding area.

My task: turn a new technology into an understandable use case.

In an earlier professional role, I was responsible for the technical design and project coordination of this application. This involved bringing together iBeacon's capabilities, the intended content and the visitor journey.

Testing at the venue was equally important. Radio signals and spatial conditions cannot be assessed solely from a desk. The mapping between stand areas and information therefore had to be checked and fine-tuned on site.

Align technology and the exhibition experience.

The essential work lay in connecting spatial context, a mobile application and understandable information presentation.

  1. 01

    Match areas and content meaningfully

    First, it was necessary to clarify which information was relevant to visitors in each part of the stand. This provided the domain basis for mapping stand areas to mobile content.

  2. 02

    Use iBeacon as a proximity signal

    Using iBeacon signals, the application could detect proximity to defined areas and provide related content. What mattered was a comprehensible use of the signal, not a claim of pinpoint indoor positioning.

  3. 03

    Test the application at the exhibition stand

    The mobile solution was developed, tested at the venue and adjusted to the spatial conditions. The focus was on whether the combination of signal and information worked in actual use.

From stand area to relevant content.

The principle is not to record a visitor's entire route. What matters is that the app detects proximity to an area and can display relevant content.

A mobile application for an internal exhibition.

The design and development work produced an iBeacon-supported exhibition application that was used at an internal event. The mapping between stand areas and content could thus be tested in its intended setting rather than considered only as a technical idea.

Its use received positive feedback. It made the connection between spatial context and mobile content tangible as a specific user experience.

What this experience means for my work today.

New technologies are interesting when they solve a real problem or make an experience noticeably easier to understand. This requires knowing the context, taking technical limitations seriously and testing the solution under real conditions.

I bring this perspective to technology decisions at mehr.wert today: the focus is on value and whether a tool works reliably in its particular setting, rather than on novelty.
