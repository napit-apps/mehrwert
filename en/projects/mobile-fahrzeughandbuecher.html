PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE · MARCEL RUSS

Digital vehicle manuals

Mercedes-Benz Guides

Making extensive vehicle information available on mobile devices – through software development, content tools, internationalisation and technical project management.

Mobile product development · iOS & Android · Personal professional experience across different project phases

Technical knowledge should be easily accessible on the go.

Vehicle manuals contain many functions, instructions and technical details. As a mobile application, this information should be understandable and reliably available – on different devices, for different vehicles and in international markets.

This involves more than developing a user interface. Content needs to be maintained, prepared for different languages, delivered technically and tested across app versions. For such products to work, app development, content processes and quality assurance must work together.

From mobile development to technical project management.

I worked on the digital vehicle manuals at different stages of my career. Initially, at CGI, I was involved in developing mobile applications and technical foundations. In a later phase, at inloop, I took on technical project management for further development into MBGuides 2.0.

My responsibility evolved with this development: from implementation, technical questions and quality assurance to coordinating app and content solutions, coordinating development and supporting releases. This also included working with a nearshore team in Slovakia.

For a mobile manual to work, the content's journey must work too.

Three technical tasks particularly shaped the work on these applications.

  1. 01

    Mobile applications and reliable technology

    Development and architecture for mobile applications, automated tests and handling locally stored content were part of the project setting. The aim was a technical foundation for presenting extensive vehicle information clearly and developing it further.

  2. 02

    Internationalisation and content tools

    Content for different languages requires dedicated technical solutions. These included support for right-to-left scripts and Python-based localisation automation. Work also covered tools for maintaining and providing manual content.

  3. 03

    Coordinating distributed development and quality

    The later project phase added technical coordination between the app and workbench, coordination of a development team in Slovakia, and quality and release topics. This meant not only implementing features, but jointly managing several technical and organisational parts of a product.

From maintained content to the mobile application.

Digital vehicle manuals connect editorially maintained content with technical delivery to the app. The individual steps must fit together.

What emerged from the collaboration.

The result was not just a single mobile feature, but work on a product with several technical components and international requirements.

Digital vehicle manuals for mobile platforms

The applications made extensive vehicle information available on mobile devices. The release of MBGuides 2.0 for iOS and Android is documented for the later project phase.

Tools and workflows for international content

Content tools, localisation automation and technical solutions for different scripts supported the processing of international manual content.

Connecting engineering and technical leadership

My responsibilities included hands-on development as well as later technical project management and collaboration with a nearshore team. These perspectives have shaped my understanding of complex software products.

What I bring from this experience today.

A complex software product is not created through code alone. Architecture, content, tools, tests and teamwork need to fit together. Working on several international product versions in particular reveals how technical decisions and organisational questions influence each other.

This experience is part of the technical foundation of my work at mehr.wert today. In strategic decisions, I can look beyond target visions and assess the consequences for implementation, operation, quality and collaboration.

Making technology decisions with the whole product in mind?

Whether existing applications are being developed further or new technical requirements arise: good decisions also consider the people, tools and processes behind the software.
