PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE · MARCEL RUSS

Capture and coordinate delivery orders on mobile devices

A mobile prototype that makes information from daily delivery work useful for dispatch, developed with different device platforms and real technical constraints in mind.

Logistics · Proof of concept · Xamarin · iOS and Windows Phone

Delivery information is generated on the road. Planning happens centrally.

A logistics provider wanted to capture delivery-order information on mobile devices and make it easier to understand for central dispatch. Data from work on the road had to connect with the perspective of the planning function.

Alongside usability, the technical context was crucial: the application needed to run on different mobile platforms and incorporate location information without burdening devices with unnecessarily intensive GPS use.

My task: turn the need into a technically testable solution.

In an earlier professional role, I took responsibility for the concept and technical coordination of the initiative. This included coordination with the client, structuring mobile workflows and coordinating cross-platform development with the partners involved.

My focus was on bringing domain requirements and technical decisions together early. A prototype was intended not merely to illustrate an idea, but to show how the essential functions could work together under the intended conditions.

A mobile solution that also considers technical limitations.

The proof of concept had to bring together three topics: handling delivery orders, mobile location information and a shared technical foundation for multiple platforms.

  1. 01

    Clarify the order and information flow

    Together with those involved, we examined which information was captured on the road and what dispatch needed for coordination. This domain structure formed the basis of the mobile prototype.

  2. 02

    Develop across platforms

    The mobile implementation used Xamarin for iOS and Windows Phone. This allowed key functions for two platforms relevant at the time to be designed and technically coordinated together.

  3. 03

    Use location information judiciously

    Integrating GPS was part of the technical design. The value of location information had to be weighed against mobile devices' energy consumption, an important constraint for applications used on the road.

Connect mobile capture and central dispatch.

The prototype connected the mobile workflow with the information needed for coordination.

A proof of concept as a basis for further development.

The concept and cross-platform work produced a mobile proof of concept for capturing and coordinating delivery orders. It brought the essential mobile functions and the central dispatch perspective together in a testable solution.

The prototype was handed over to the client for internal development. This achieved the specific result of the initiative: a technical foundation for subsequent decisions and implementation steps.

What this experience taught me about software projects.

A good technical idea must fit the devices, users and operating conditions. In everyday mobile use, energy consumption and platform differences are among the questions that must be clarified early.

This perspective still shapes my work at mehr.wert: before an idea becomes a large initiative, it is worth checking key assumptions and aligning technical possibilities with actual requirements.
